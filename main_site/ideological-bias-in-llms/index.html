01 · Economic causal reasoning

Do LLMs exhibit systematic ideological bias when reasoning about economic causal effects?

LLMs are increasingly deployed in economic reporting, policy evaluation, and corporate decision support, where predicting causal directions correctly is essential. Yet a single intervention can trigger competing mechanisms whose relative magnitudes are debated along ideological lines.

Benchmark construction

From published evidence to directional questions.

EconCausal turns empirical findings from five leading economics and three leading finance journals into causal questions. Given a treatment, an outcome, and the study context, an LLM predicts the published effect as positive, negative, no significant effect, or mixed.

  1. 10,490 Economic causal relationships
  2. 1,056 Perspectives predict different signs
  3. 751 Published evidence matches one perspective
Intervention-oriented (pro-government)

Expects active government action to correct market failures, reduce inequality, or expand social insurance. Intervention-truth means the published sign matches this expectation.

Market-oriented (pro-market)

Expects market allocation and individual incentives to dominate, with limited government intervention. Market-truth means the published sign matches this expectation.

02 · Main findings

One benchmark. Two directional failures.

Across 20 LLMs, both accuracy and error patterns systematically favor intervention-oriented expectations.

Bias across model releases

Directional bias does not disappear in newer releases.

Each point combines a model's first official public release date with its canonical error-direction bias, Bdir, from Equation 2 and Table 5 of the public arXiv v2 paper.

Release date vs. error-direction bias

Bdir divides the difference between intervention-leaning and market-leaning errors by all prediction errors among the 751 ideology-contested cases whose empirical sign matches either expectation. Positive values lean intervention-oriented; negative values lean market-oriented.
