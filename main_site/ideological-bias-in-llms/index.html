Ideological Bias in LLMs' Economic Causal Reasoning

When economic mechanisms disagree, LLM reliability tilts.

We test whether language models reason equally well when empirically supported economic effects align with intervention-oriented versus market-oriented expectations.

01 · The question

Not what a model says it believes.
Where its reasoning becomes less reliable.

Political-compass tests measure expressed preferences. We instead compare model predictions against causal signs supported by empirical studies.

Typical probe

“What do you think?”

→

02 · Building the benchmark

From published evidence to a direction-aware test

A compact pipeline turns EconCausal into a controlled test of ideological asymmetry.

  1. 1 10,490 EconCausal triplets Context, treatment, outcome, and empirical sign
  2. 2 4 families assign expected signs GPT, Claude, Qwen, and Grok; blinded to the result
  3. 3 1,056 candidate-contested triplets Intervention and market expectations diverge
  4. 4 898 ideology-sensitive cases Used for contested vs. non-contested accuracy
  5. 5 751 direction-aligned cases 436 intervention-truth · 315 market-truth
  6. 6 20 LLMs evaluated Accuracy gap + directional error bias

Metric scopes: aggregate contested accuracy uses 898 ideology-sensitive cases (751 direction-aligned + 147 sensitive-neither); direction-aware accuracy and error metrics use the 751 direction-aligned cases.

✓
Expert validation Two economics professors reviewed 126 balanced cases: 93.60% / 98.35% accuracy and 93.33% inter-rater agreement.

06 · Can one example fix it?

One-shot prompting moves answers—but does not reliably remove the asymmetry.

The mitigation test is deliberately simple: prepend one intervention-oriented or market-oriented demonstration, then re-evaluate the same targets.

Scope: this result concerns one-shot examples; it does not rule out stronger multi-shot, system-prompt, or training-based mitigation.

07 · Why it matters

Average accuracy can hide directional failure.

For policy analysis, economic reporting, and decision support, evaluate not only whether a model is correct—but whether its reliability changes across contested directions.

Caveats & open questions +

Labels. Four LLM families construct the ideology-conditioned labels; human validation covers a balanced 126-case sample.

Evidence distribution. Topic, publication, and context selection in the empirical literature may shape the benchmark's ground-truth distribution.

Mechanism. Proprietary training pipelines prevent cleanly separating pretraining, alignment, and family-specific post-training effects.

Reasoning vs. recall. Future work should distinguish paper-specific retrieval from mechanism-based causal reasoning.
