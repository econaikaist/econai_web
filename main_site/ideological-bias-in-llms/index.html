01 · Economic causal reasoning

Do LLMs exhibit systematic ideological bias when reasoning about economic causal effects?

LLMs are increasingly deployed in economic reporting, policy evaluation, and corporate decision support, where predicting causal directions correctly is essential. Yet a single intervention can trigger competing mechanisms whose relative magnitudes are debated along ideological lines.

02 · Main findings

One benchmark. Two directional failures.

Across 20 LLMs, both accuracy and error patterns systematically favor intervention-oriented expectations.

Built from published economic evidence

EconCausal contains 10,490 treatment–outcome relationships extracted from empirical studies in top economics and finance journals, each with its observed effect direction. Four LLMs, blinded to that result, predict the direction expected from intervention- and market-oriented perspectives. This yields 1,056 disagreements; the main analysis uses the 751 whose observed direction matches one expectation.

The asymmetry is concentrated, not uniform.

04 · One-shot prompting

A single example does not reliably steer answers.

We prepend one semantically similar example whose answer points in the correct ideological direction, then compare each model with its no-example baseline.
