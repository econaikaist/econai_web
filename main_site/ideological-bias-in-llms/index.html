Ideological Bias in LLMs' Economic Causal Reasoning

Donggyu Lee1, Hyeok Yun2, Jungwon Kim2, Junsik Min3, Sungwon Park3,*, Sangyoon Park4,*, Jihee Kim2,*

1Graduate School of Data Science, KAIST 2College of Business, KAIST 3School of Computing, KAIST 4Division of Social Science, HKUST *Co-corresponding authors

Paper PDF arXiv ↗

Do LLMs exhibit systematic ideological bias when reasoning about economic causal effects?

LLMs are increasingly deployed in economic reporting, policy evaluation, and corporate decision support, where predicting causal directions correctly is essential. Yet a single intervention can trigger competing mechanisms whose relative magnitudes are debated along ideological lines.

Benchmark construction

From published evidence to directional questions.

EconCausal is a dataset of causal relationships extracted from economics and finance journals. Each record includes a treatment, an outcome, the study context, and the empirical effect sign. Among 10,490 records, intervention-oriented and market-oriented perspectives predict different signs for 1,056. The directional analysis uses the 878 cases whose empirical sign aligns with one of the two perspectives: 507 intervention-truth and 371 market-truth cases. The remaining 178 contested cases align with neither perspective.

  1. 10,490 Economic causal relationships
  2. 1,056 Perspectives predict different signs
  3. 878 Empirical sign matches one perspective
Intervention-oriented (pro-government, left)

Expects active government action to correct market failures, reduce inequality, or expand social insurance. Intervention-truth means the empirical sign matches that ideology-conditioned expectation.

Market-oriented (pro-market, right)

Expects market allocation and individual incentives to dominate, with limited government intervention. Market-truth means the empirical sign matches that ideology-conditioned expectation.
